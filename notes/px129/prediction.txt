px129 — the console draws full-screen programs slowly on the installed machine: the prediction,
written before the instrument, before any change (2026-11-12).

What is known before measuring
- The installed machine: a 2560 x 1440 UEFI GOP framebuffer, 32 bpp, pitch 16384, at physical
  0x4000000000 (its /proc/pax-boot, read as an ordinary user). The grid is 256 x 90 cells at scale 1
  (the screen doubles glyphs only above 2560 pixels wide), so a full screen is 23,040 cells.
- A cell is 8 x 16 pixels of 4 bytes: 512 bytes of framebuffer a cell, 11.8 MB a full screen. The
  screen draws a cell as 16 pixel rows of four 64-bit stores, each row a different framebuffer line
  (pitch apart); it never reads the framebuffer (px87).
- Every byte a program writes to the console goes through serial.put one byte at a time: the
  screen's put (interrupts off, two RDTSCs, the parser, then the cursor drawn again when shown), the
  boot log's copy, then COM1 when a UART answered at boot (a poll of LSR before each byte).
- Every cell a write touches is drawn, whether or not its pixels change: a program that rewrites
  the whole screen for one keypress draws every cell it rewrites. ED 2 draws every cell blank, then
  the repaint draws them again. A scroll on the metal (px87's redraw way) draws every cell whose
  value moved, which for a screen of text is nearly every cell: one scrolled line costs about one
  full screen. With the cursor shown, a printed character draws two cells (itself and the cursor
  after it).
- kernel/fb maps the framebuffer write-combining when the CPU has the PAT and IA32_PAT entry 5 is
  WC (Limine's layout), else uncached. The boot's `screen:` line says which; it is root's to read on
  the installed machine.
- px125 measured about 10 ms a full screen under KVM at 160 x 50 and 256 x 90 alike, timed on
  CLOCK_MONOTONIC, which moves in 10 ms ticks on PAX (pax#299): the number is one tick, not a cost.

The instrument (user/elf/conspeed.c): one command at the console, a table on stdout. Tests: bytes
(parse only, nothing drawn), cell (one cell a write), plain (a), sgr (b), scroll1 and scroll10 (c),
clear (d), cursor (plain with the cursor shown), and the recorded streams (e): yew's F mode
keypresses and cub's frames. Timed with RDTSC against CLOCK_MONOTONIC over the whole run.

Predictions for the installed machine (P1-P8)
P1 Its COM1: absent (the shape px76 and px87 recorded for this machine). The bytes test's cost a
   byte is CPU work alone, under 1 us (not the 87 us a byte a polled 16550 at 115200 baud costs).
P2 The framebuffer is write-combining (kernel/fb's choice holds on this CPU). The cost a cell drawn,
   plain less its bytes, is 0.5-3 us, about 170-1000 MB/s of pixels. If it is uncached instead, a
   cell costs 5 us or more (under 100 MB/s) and that alone is the slowness.
P3 plain (a): 15-70 ms a full screen; sgr (b) 1.2-2x plain (nine bytes a cell to parse, the same
   cells drawn).
P4 scroll1 (c): within 0.7-1.3x plain a line (every cell of a text screen moves); scroll10 ten times
   that. This is the worst case a person sees (cat of a file, a shell's output) and a scroll of a
   screenful costs about rows x plain: seconds.
P5 clear (d): about 2x plain (every cell drawn blank, then drawn again).
P6 cursor: about 2x plain (two cells drawn a character).
P7 yew's F mode keypress (e): it rewrites most of the screen for one moved selection, so it costs
   about a full screen of plain or sgr, tens of ms, where the cells that change are a few hundred:
   about 20-100x more drawing than the keypress needs. cub's frame: a full coloured screen, about
   sgr's cost.
P8 QEMU under KVM (the framebuffer is host memory, written back cached): the cost a cell is the
   CPU's alone, 3-20x less than the metal's; plain under 10 ms at 256 x 90. With COM1 present (the
   harness's serial log), every byte is two port accesses QEMU serves in user space, so the bytes
   test and every test with it are dominated by the UART under KVM: the comparison with the metal
   is made with COM1 absent.

What the fix is predicted to be (to be confirmed or replaced by the numbers)
F1 Draw only cells whose drawn value changes: the grid is already the shadow; a second grid of what
   the framebuffer shows lets a write draw a cell only when it differs. yew's keypress and cub's
   frame drop to the cells that change (P7's 20-100x), clear plus repaint to one pass, a repeated
   identical frame to nothing.
F2 Draw at the end of the write, not per byte: a write updates the grid and the cursor in RAM and
   draws the changed cells once when it ends (and the kernel's own lines likewise). A scroll of ten
   lines in one write draws its final screen once (scroll10 drops by about ten), the cursor's
   per-character redraw disappears (cursor drops to plain).
F3 A cell drawn row-major and branch-free: a pixel row of a run of changed cells at a time (whole
   64-byte write-combining lines, sequential), each glyph row expanded through a 256-entry table of
   eight pixels' masks rather than a division and two tests a pixel pair. plain and sgr drop by the
   CPU's share (P8's QEMU number) and by the write-combining gain on the metal.
F4 The 256-column cap: the grid's storage is fixed at 256 x 128 cells a screen; lifting it to 320
   needs larger static grids (and a second, drawn, grid for F1). Predicted cheap enough to lift with
   F1 if the storage grows by under 1 MiB.

Rows (red at trunk, green after; ratios on one boot, never absolutes)
R1 a repeated identical full frame costs at most 1/4 of a changing one (red at trunk: about 1).
R2 scroll10 costs at most 3x scroll1 (red at trunk: about 10x).
R3 clear plus repaint costs at most 1.3x the repaint alone (red at trunk: about 2x).
R4 cursor shown costs at most 1.3x hidden (red at trunk: about 2x).
R5 yew's recorded F mode keypress and cub's frame each cost at most a bound set from the cells
   they change (red at trunk: the cells they write).
R6 every existing screen row (tools/screen-ref, mpx3-screen, mpx5-screen, mpx6-games, mpx3-console)
   draws byte-identical pixels.
