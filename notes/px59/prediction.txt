px59 — The console for an editor

Contract: wolf/sprints/pax/59-the-console-for-an-editor/px59-the-console-for-an-editor.md.
Base: origin/px57 0681c57e; trunk ebbef867. This prediction precedes
any change to the kernel, renderer, probes, suites, and any VT measurement.
Clean-room: console_codes(4), termios(3), ECMA-48, the Linux VT as a
black box; PAX's own implementation. No Linux source, no yew edits.
Builds in px13-ubuntu on kasumi, QEMU taskset -c 0-3, ulimit -c 0.

P1. tools/screen-ref knows text, UTF-8, deferred wrap, CR/LF/BS/TAB,
SGR 0/1/7/30-37/39/40-47/49/90-97 and an always-visible inverse
cursor. It knows none of the requested cursor addressing, erase,
save/restore, visibility/shape, alternate screen, OSC or query classes.

P2. A separate alternate cell buffer at the existing four bytes per
cell costs 32 frames at the maximum 256x128 grid, eight frames at
50x160. Metadata fits in a frame. Allocation need not happen while
parsing an escape. The Linux VT likely ignores ?1049 and DECSCUSR;
if measured, explicit alternate-screen support is a PAX divergence
requiring R-px59-1 rather than a claim of VT parity.

P3. Linux VT DA1 answers ESC[?6c; CSI 6n answers its current 1-based
row and column in ESC[r;cR; kitty and DECRQM 2026 stay unanswered.
The serial device must not receive a duplicate answer when the host's
terminal already answers: C_SRC (last input device) must gate replies.
A query must enter input without changing the last input device.

P4. Function keys F1-F5 give ESC[[A through ESC[[E, F6-F12 give
ESC[17~ ESC[18~ ESC[19~ ESC[20~ ESC[21~ ESC[23~ ESC[24~.
Modified arrows probably do not all use CSI 1;m X on the Linux VT;
measure each modifier before selecting an encoding. Left Alt prefixes
ESC to printable keys; right Alt may be AltGr and needs its own row.

P5. Cursor addressing and SGR 22/27 will remove yew's first-frame
smear, but cell-for-cell agreement needs the reference parser changed
alongside the kernel and keyboard-mode 50x160 input. The Linux VT
cannot be treated as an xterm: alternate buffers, shape and modified
keys are the predicted weak points. Native and release, BIOS and UEFI
must agree; every new assertion needs a CI plant before acceptance.

Plants to define by class after measurements, before implementation:
restore ignored cursor addressing, SGR 22/27, OSC text leakage,
missing queries, or missing keyboard classes; each one-leg dispatch
must cite its exact red row. Existing old dropped-escape rows need
explicit served/divergent replacements, not removed assertions.

Mac. px56 records build.sh and assemble.sh on kasumi. No assembly or
maintainer-session claim until the correct inputs and destination are
confirmed and their scripts read.

Addendum (px59, the third agent, 2026-10-21; committed before the
second VT measurement, notes/px59/vtprobe2.c, and before any change to
kernel/console or kernel/screen's queries):

P6. The arrows take every modifier as CSI 1;m X with m = 1 + Shift 1 +
Alt 2 + Ctrl 4 (vt.rows has 2, 3, 5, 6 for up), unless the VT's own
keymap takes the combination first: Alt+Left and Alt+Right may switch
consoles (no bytes). Home, End, PgUp, PgDn, Insert and Delete keep
their unmodified CSI n ~ under Shift and Ctrl (no CSI n;m ~); Shift with
PgUp or PgDn gives nothing (the VT's own scrolling).

P7. Shift+F1 to Shift+F8 give the codes of F11 to F20 (CSI 23~ 24~ 25~
26~ 28~ 29~ 31~ 32~), Shift+F9 to F12 CSI 33~ 34~ or nothing; Ctrl+Fn
the unmodified Fn; Alt+F1 nothing (a console switch, to tty1 itself).

P8. Left Alt with a printable key, Enter, Backspace, Tab, Esc and Space
gives ESC then what the key gives without Alt (so Alt+Shift+a ESC A,
Ctrl+Alt+a ESC 01, Alt+Backspace ESC 7f, Alt+Enter ESC 0d).

P9. The VT answers ESC Z as DA1 (ESC[?6c), CSI 0 c as CSI c, CSI 5 n
with ESC[0n, and CSI 6 n with the cursor's row and column, the last
column itself with a wrap pending, the alternate screen's own position
on it; DA2 (CSI > c), DECXCPR (CSI ? 6 n) and OSC 11's query get no
answer; yew's probe string gets DA1's answer alone. In canonical mode
with ECHO and ECHOCTL the answer is echoed (^[[?6c on the screen), as
typed bytes are.

P10. SGR 2, 3 and 4 are drawn on the VGA VT as colours (vcsa shows a
changed attribute), 5 as the blink bit; 38;2 and 48;2 map to the
nearest of the 16; a ninth CSI parameter does not end CUP on the VT
(PAX ignores such a sequence: a divergence to name); ICH, DCH, ECH,
IL, DL and DECSTBM are served by the VT (not yew's: listed, not
implemented here); ?1049h clears the alternate screen in the current
background; a scroll on either screen leaves the other as it was.
