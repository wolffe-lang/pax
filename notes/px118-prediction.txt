px118 — a boot that goes black leaves evidence. The prediction, written before the first change.

Every row below is QEMU only (SeaBIOS and OVMF); nothing here is a claim about real hardware.

1. The beeps. A console kernel boot shows two tones in QEMU's PC-speaker capture (the `wav`
   audio backend) before its first program: the first at the kernel's entry, the second when the
   console has drawn its first line, each a fixed pitch and length (the first higher and
   shorter), each timed by the speaker's own counter (port 0x61 bit 5), so the wait is the
   beep. With `pax.nobeep` on the command line the capture holds no boot tone. With no
   framebuffer the capture holds the first tone only. A boot gains about a quarter of a second.
   The speaker's own books (its tones count, its ring) do not move, so no session's transcript
   gains a `speaker:` line.

2. Limine says more. With `verbose: yes`, `serial: yes` and a five-second menu in the stick's
   and the installed configuration (no `quiet: yes`), QEMU's serial log shows Limine's menu
   and its loading lines before PAX's first line, under both firmwares. The suites that boot
   the stick or an installed disk and read the serial log from PAX's first line still pass;
   a suite that types before PAX's first prompt is the risk (Limine reads serial input too).

3. The marks' store, measured before it is chosen for good: QEMU's CMOS keeps bytes a guest
   wrote across a guest reset (port 0xCF9, the FADT's register) and the monitor's
   system_reset, under both firmwares; neither firmware writes the bytes picked for the marks.
   A cold start of QEMU begins with them zero.

4. The early log in memory. QEMU keeps guest RAM across a guest reset and system_reset; a
   fixed physical range the kernel keeps out of its frame allocator is found intact by its
   checksum on the next boot under SeaBIOS. Under OVMF: also intact, unless the firmware or
   Limine writes the range, which the checksum then refuses (predicted: intact). A cold start
   of QEMU finds no log.

5. The stages. A kernel stopped by a test-only word at each stage leaves that stage as its
   last mark; the next boot's first program appends `boot N: previous boot stopped at STAGE`
   to /var/log/boot.log, and `completed` after a clean end. A log area with one byte changed
   is refused by its checksum and not printed. An upgrade keeps /var/log/boot.log. The stick
   boots and reaches its login unchanged when the store is absent or read-only.

6. A black hand-off in QEMU (OVMF, a GOP mode change, no EDID): predicted NOT reproduced;
   Limine and PAX draw in every configuration tried.
