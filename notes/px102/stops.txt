px102 — a non-tracer thread consuming a stop, and an untraced caller's
exit_group: the prediction, written 2026-11-06 before the witnesses have
run anywhere. Sources: ptrace(2) ("Death under ptrace", the tracer is a
thread), wait(2) (__WNOTHREAD: by default a thread waits for children of
other threads), clone(2).

consume-stop: the leader forks a TRACEME child that raises SIGSTOP; the
tracer's second thread (not the tracer) calls wait4(child, __WALL).
Predicted on Linux: it sees the stop (0x137f), consuming it; its own
GETREGS is ESRCH (-3: it is not the tracer); the leader's GETREGS is 0
and its WNOHANG wait finds nothing (0); the leader CONTs; the second
thread's next wait sees the exit (0x2500); then ECHILD.
PAX at 536c082e: "PAX lets only the tracer thread consume a stop"
(lifecycle.txt): the second thread's wait never sees the stop: RED.

exit-untraced: an untraced process whose non-leader thread alone is
ATTACHed with TRACEEXIT; the untraced leader calls exit_group. Predicted:
the attached thread stops in PTRACE_EVENT_EXIT (0x6057f), the message
its exit code, then (CONT) the thread's death and the leader's are
reported, the thread's first. PAX: "an untraced caller's exit_group still
ends the others at once, with their death reports but without their
EVENT_EXIT stops" (lifecycle.txt): RED at the event stop.

Measured on Linux (the witness commit's binaries; five byte-identical
takes per mode as uid 0 and as uid 1000, uid 0 equal to uid 1000): END
linux-consume-stop-0/-1000 and linux-exit-untraced-0/-1000, all exit=0
takes=5. References: tests/mpx5-trace.d/consume-stop.linux and
exit-untraced.linux. Every prediction held (the thread's death was
reported before the leader's: untraced.death.0.thread, .1.leader).

Red on 054a32c0's kernel (both by name, not yet fixed; the modes are
kept out of tests/mpx5-trace's list until they are):
  consume-stop   FAIL T-consume-thread-wait, T-consume-leader,
                 T-consume-exit: the second thread's wait never sees the
                 stop; the boot ran out its 120 s without a line.
  exit-untraced  FAIL T-untraced-exit-event, T-untraced-deaths: the
                 attached thread is reaped (0x2500) with no EVENT_EXIT
                 stop, as lifecycle.txt named.
Owed beside them, with no witness yet: group-stop and PTRACE_LISTEN, and
ATTACH to a job-control-stopped target.

Fixed at f1db73e0, both green (root, BIOS/KVM; frames back):
  consume-stop   wait(2) lets any thread of the tracer's process wait for
                 the tracee (kernel/sched: tracer_here); requests stay the
                 tracer thread's (the other thread's GETREGS is -ESRCH).
  exit-untraced  an untraced caller's exit_group parks every attached
                 TRACEEXIT thread in EVENT_EXIT and goes on exiting; the
                 parked threads are not ended with it, and the leader's
                 death is reported after theirs.
Both are in tests/mpx5-trace now.

tgkill of a joined thread (the mpx3-threads T1 report). Measured, not
guessed: the new witnesses tgkill-ended and tgkill-traced join a thread by
its clear-child-tid word and then tgkill/tkill its tid. On PAX the answer
is -ESRCH at the base kernel (411fc9f5's kernel and boot, with the same
programs) and at this lane's head alike: nothing here changed it. On
Linux it is a race with the exiting thread (the word is cleared before the
thread is gone): untraced, 0 in all takes on a 2-CPU slice; traced, 0 in
one take and -ESRCH in another. The mpx3-threads T1 line therefore
differs when Linux's run lands on the 0 side (the checkpoint's six CPUs).
No kernel change; the two witnesses are kept out of the suite because
Linux gives no stable reference for them.
