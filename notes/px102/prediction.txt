A. What a gdb-like debugger asks of Linux x86-64 (predicted)

Requests (the uapi numbers), by what a session does:
  start a program   fork; the child PTRACE_TRACEME (0), then execve; the
                    parent's waitpid sees the exec SIGTRAP stop
                    ((SIGTRAP << 8) | 0x7f); PTRACE_SETOPTIONS (0x4200)
                    with TRACESYSGOOD | TRACEFORK | TRACEVFORK | TRACECLONE
                    | TRACEEXEC | TRACEVFORKDONE | EXITKILL; gdb also first
                    forks throwaway children to probe which options the
                    kernel takes (TRACEME, SIGSTOP, SETOPTIONS, a fork in
                    the child, GETEVENTMSG (0x4201), KILL (8)).
  read and write    PEEKTEXT/PEEKDATA (1, 2) and POKEDATA (5) word by
  memory            word, or (a newer gdb) pread64/pwrite64 of
                    /proc/<pid>/mem; breakpoints are an 0xcc POKEd into
                    read-only text.
  registers         GETREGS (12) / SETREGS (13), or GETREGSET (0x4204)
                    NT_PRSTATUS; GETFPREGS (14) or GETREGSET NT_PRFPREG /
                    NT_X86_XSTATE (0x202) for the vector state; PEEKUSER (3)
                    of DR6 and DR7 (offsets 848 + 8 * n of struct user)
                    at stops, POKEUSER (6) for watchpoints.
  run               CONT (7), SINGLESTEP (9) (stepping off a breakpoint,
                    `step`/`next` over a line), SYSCALL (24) only for
                    `catch syscall`; KILL (8) or kill(SIGKILL) at the end.
  stops             waitpid(-1 or pid, __WALL | WNOHANG) after a SIGCHLD
                    (a handler and sigsuspend), GETSIGINFO (0x4202) at a
                    SIGTRAP to tell a breakpoint (si_code SI_KERNEL 0x80)
                    from a step (TRAP_TRACE 2) or a watchpoint (TRAP_HWBKPT
                    4); the event stops' status >> 16 (PTRACE_EVENT_*).
  attach            ATTACH (16) or SEIZE (0x4206) + INTERRUPT (0x4207);
                    DETACH (17).
The wait statuses: an exit (code << 8), a signal death (sig), a ptrace
stop (sig << 8) | 0x7f, a syscall stop ((SIGTRAP | 0x80) << 8) | 0x7f
under TRACESYSGOOD, an event stop (((PTRACE_EVENT_x << 8) | SIGTRAP) << 8)
| 0x7f, PTRACE_EVENT_STOP 128 for SEIZE's group-stops and INTERRUPT.

Which sniff needs when:
  S2 (the core): TRACEME + exec stop, SETOPTIONS (TRACEEXEC, TRACESYSGOOD,
     EXITKILL; the TRACE{FORK,VFORK,CLONE} bits accepted), PEEK/POKEDATA
     and /proc/<pid>/mem read and write, GETREGS/SETREGS, GETFPREGS,
     PEEKUSER of DR6/DR7 (reads 0 with no watchpoint), CONT, SINGLESTEP
     (including over `syscall`), KILL, GETSIGINFO, waitpid with __WALL and
     WNOHANG, SIGCHLD to the tracer at every stop, TRACEEXIT's
     GETEVENTMSG, the exit and death reports.
  S5 (watchpoints): POKEUSER of DR0-DR3 and DR7 validated as Linux does,
     DR6's status bits after a hit, TRAP_HWBKPT, PEEKUSER of DR6 cleared
     by the tracer.
  S6 (threads, signals, attach): ATTACH, SEIZE, INTERRUPT, DETACH,
     TRACECLONE of CLONE_THREAD children and their per-thread stops,
     group-stop and LISTEN, signal injection and suppression across
     threads, GETEVENTMSG of clone and exec.
  S7 (cores): not ptrace; PAX writing core(5) files (its own lane).

B. The kernel paths each leans on (PAX at 411fc9f5)

  - kernel/user `serve`: the dispatch (101 is -ENOSYS today); the
    syscall-entry and -exit stops wrap it; exit/exit_group for TRACEEXIT.
  - kernel/signal `on_return`, `act`, `fault`, `die`: where a traced
    thread's taken signal becomes a signal-delivery-stop instead of an
    action, and where the injected signal is acted on after the resume.
  - kernel/interrupts `route`: vector 3 from ring 3 is today a #GP (every
    gate is DPL 0) that kills with SIGSEGV; vector 1 from ring 3 kills
    "debug". Both become SIGTRAP; gate 3 is made DPL 3; #DB goes on an
    IST stack (a SYSCALL with TF set may raise #DB at the entry, on the
    user's stack, in ring 0).
  - kernel/sched: a new thread state for a ptrace-stop (parked with its
    frame, run only when the tracer restarts it or SIGKILL), the trace
    words per thread (tracer, options, the stop's report, the event
    message, the stopped signal and its origin, the debug registers),
    `wait_find` taking a tracer's tracees as well as its children, SIGCHLD
    and a wake for the tracer at every stop, the tracer's end detaching
    its tracees, a traced child's death reported to the tracer first.
  - kernel/process `wait4`/`waitid` (__WALL, the stop reports, CLD_TRAPPED),
    `execve` (the exec stop or the legacy SIGTRAP), `clone` (fork, vfork
    and clone events and auto-attach).
  - kernel/fpu: a stopped thread's saved area read as user_fpregs_struct
    (the FXSAVE image; XSAVE's init-state bits honoured).
  - kernel/paging, kernel/vm, kernel/uaccess: a tracee's memory through
    its own tables, written even where ring 3 may not (breakpoints into
    r-x text), a file's page copied before a private write.
  - kernel/files and kernel/procfs: `may_trace` reused with the real ids
    (PTRACE_MODE_ATTACH_REALCREDS) and with the file-system ids (ATTACH_
    FSCREDS for /proc/<pid>/mem); /proc/<pid>/mem read and written;
    `status`'s TracerPid and State "t (tracing stop)".
  - boot/isr.S `pax_sysret`: a frame with TF set leaves by IRETQ.
  - boot/cpu.S: MOV to and from DR0-DR3, DR6, DR7; kernel/sched loads a
    thread's at every switch in and clears DR7 for one with none.

C. The rows, predicted (tests/mpx5-trace: a static no-libc tracer,
   user/elf/trace.c, and an assembly tracee, user/elf/tracee.S, run on
   PAX as root and as 1000:1000 and in a throwaway Linux container,
   compared byte for byte)

  T1 base + the suite: red. Every ptrace call answers -38 (ENOSYS) and the
     kernel logs `syscall 101: -ENOSYS`; the child's TRACEME fails, its
     execve runs untraced, and its int3 is a #GP killed as SIGSEGV
     (status 0xb), where Linux's is a SIGTRAP stop at the breakpoint.
  T2 attach and stop: the exec stop is (SIGTRAP << 8) | 0x7f = 0x57f;
     with TRACEEXEC, 0x4057f; the int3 stop 0x57f with rip one past the
     0xcc; GETSIGINFO si_signo 5, si_code 0x80 (SI_KERNEL) for the int3,
     2 (TRAP_TRACE) for a step; TracerPid the tracer's pid while traced
     and 0 after; State "t (tracing stop)" while stopped.
  T3 memory: PEEKDATA of the tracee's text and data words equal the bytes
     the tracer reads from its own copy of the image; POKEDATA of 0xcc
     into r-x text succeeds; /proc/<pid>/mem pread and pwrite at an
     address agree with PEEK/POKE; an unmapped address is -EIO on both
     (Linux may say EFAULT: whatever Linux says, PAX says).
  T4 registers: GETREGS's 27 words in user_regs_struct's order (r15 …
     rdi, orig_rax, rip, cs, eflags, rsp, ss, fs_base, gs_base, ds, es,
     fs, gs); cs and ss differ by name (PAX 0x23/0x1b, Linux 0x33/0x2b:
     the transcript prints them normalised, R-px102 on selectors);
     SETREGS of rdi is what the tracee then prints; GETFPREGS's fcw
     0x037f and mxcsr 0x1f80 at exec; PEEKUSER of DR7 0, POKEUSER of DR7
     with an execute breakpoint at a function stops there with DR6's B0
     set and si_code 4; an invalid DR7 (a 2-byte execute breakpoint, RW =
     2 I/O, a misaligned 8-byte watch) answers what Linux answers.
  T5 single-step: n steps from the breakpoint report n consecutive rips,
     each a SIGTRAP stop with TRAP_TRACE; a step over `syscall` stops at
     the next instruction with the call done (rax its result), not one
     further.
  T6 syscall stops: under TRACESYSGOOD the entry stop is 0x857f with rax
     -38 and orig_rax the number, the exit stop 0x857f with rax the result.
  T7 permission: a second tracer's ATTACH -EPERM; ATTACH to itself
     -EPERM; to a non-dumpable process (PR_SET_DUMPABLE 0) -EPERM to a
     user, 0 to root; a user's ATTACH to a descendant with other ids
     -EPERM; a request to a running tracee -ESRCH; a tracer's end
     detaches its tracee, which runs on and exits by itself; EXITKILL's
     tracee dies of SIGKILL with its tracer.
  T8 events: TRACEEXIT's stop is 0x6057f with GETEVENTMSG the exit
     status word; TRACEFORK's 0x1057f with GETEVENTMSG the child's pid,
     the child stopped by SIGSTOP (0x137f).
  T9 refused by name, each its own `~` row (PAX's answer held to its text,
     Linux's printed beside it): PTRACE_SYSEMU (31), GETREGSET
     NT_X86_XSTATE (0x202), PTRACE_ARCH_PRCTL (30), and whatever else the
     measurement shows gdb asks and S2 does not need.
  T10 the kernel's books: every frame back at the end, no -ENOSYS for 101,
     no PANIC, init's end status Linux's.

D. What I expect to be hard or to differ
  - SYSCALL with TF set: whether QEMU (TCG and KVM) raises #DB at the
    entry point; either way the step after a syscall is the kernel's
    (it stops the tracee on the call's way out), and #DB on its own IST
    stack makes the entry case harmless.
  - orig_rax at a stop after an interrupt is not deterministic on Linux
    (an irq's negated vector); the transcript prints it only at syscall
    and exception stops.
  - ASLR: Linux randomises the stack of an ET_EXEC tracee and PAX does
    not: rsp and every stack address are printed relative to the
    tracee's own reading of its rsp at start (named in the row).
  - PAX's tracer is a process (any of its threads may ask); Linux's is
    the attaching thread. Named, not tested (one-thread tracers).
