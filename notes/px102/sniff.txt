px102 — what sniff can rely on, and what it still needs (item 6), as of
the head named in the hand-back. Every statement below has a mode in
tests/mpx5-trace held byte-for-byte to Linux (or, where named, to PAX's own
transcript of a refusal).

S2 can rely on (break, step, print, continue, exit):
  TRACEME and ATTACH/SEIZE/INTERRUPT/DETACH; exec stops; int3 and
  single-step stops (SIGTRAP codes as Linux); CONT/SINGLESTEP/SYSCALL with
  signal injection and replacement; GETREGS/SETREGS (with the round trip of
  everything PAX reports), GETFPREGS, GETREGSET PRSTATUS/PRFPREG;
  PEEK/POKE of text and data (a breakpoint into read-only text works),
  /proc/<pid>/mem read and write, PROT_NONE and private-file pages as
  Linux; wait statuses with the event bits; TracerPid; permission checks
  (dumpable, credentials).
S5 (hardware watchpoints): DR0-DR3 and DR7 through POKEUSER, DR6 through
  PEEKUSER, validated as Linux validates; a real write watchpoint stop
  (SI code 4). Still needed: nothing known for write watchpoints; read
  (RW=3) and execute breakpoints follow DR7's validation, but only the
  write case has a PAX row with a hardware hit.
S6 (threads): TRACECLONE with auto-attach, individual thread deaths, the
  group's death and EVENT_EXIT, a non-leader's exec, the tracer as a thread,
  any tracer-process thread waiting, task/<tid>/mem and task/<tid>/status.
  Still needed: task/<tid>/stat, per-thread registers of a thread the
  debugger found through task/ (it works for a traced thread), PTRACE_O_
  tracing of a thread's SIGSTOP stop ordering under load.
S7 (core dumps): nothing from ptrace; the cores themselves are PAX's
  (kernel writes none; core_pattern is a fixed "core").
Refused, and what that costs:
  SETFPREGS/SETREGSET (restoring FP/vector state after an inferior call),
  SETSIGINFO and PEEKSIGINFO (rare), GET_SYSCALL_INFO (a newer gdb prefers
  it; the old SYSCALL-stop registers still work), SYSEMU, SINGLEBLOCK,
  LISTEN and group-stop reporting (R-px102-4), non-canonical/NT/GS-base
  register writes (R-px102-3), NT_X86_XSTATE (AVX state).
Not covered: maps, auxv, and the gdb-on-PAX session itself (sn00's).

Security note: PAX applies ptrace(2)'s access-mode checks without Yama; same-uid
processes may trace and read each other, as Linux does at ptrace_scope 0 (a
uid-1000 process can open another uid-1000 process's /proc/<pid>/mem and
ATTACH to it, descendant or not). Yama is a later item.
