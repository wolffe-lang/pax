# auferstehung-end: the reduction (px29)

Work: Gustav Mahler, Symphony No. 2 in C minor, fifth movement (finale).
Source: the full score's first edition, Leipzig: Friedrich Hofmeister,
1897 (University of Toronto Music Library, M1001 .M21 no. 2 1897),
scanned by the Internet Archive as `symphonieno200mahl`: the PDF
(sha256 cab6fa78…) and its page images `symphonieno200mahl_jp2.zip`,
images 0206 (printed p. 204) and 0211 (printed p. 209). Public domain:
published 1897; the composer died in 1911. Nothing else was used: no
sound file of a performance, no encoding, no later edition.

How it was read: each staff cropped from the page image with its five
lines found and drawn over it, and the noteheads' staff positions
located by script (`staff position -> pitch` in the clef and key) and
by eye on the enlarged crop; every note of the tune was checked that
way.

## Not verified

- **51, the last bar**: the strings' chord was located by script at
  B2/B2, G5, B4, F3 on staves whose clefs were not confirmed in the
  crop; those readings are not musically consistent with the E-flat
  close, so they are taken as misreads and the bar is sounded with the
  organ's chord. The strings' pitches there are not verified.
- **51, the brass**: read only as far as their rhythm; not carried.
- **The organ's top note in bars 9-12**: read as Eb6 (a ledger-line
  note above the chord) from one crop at 7x; the rest of the chord
  as Eb5 G5 Bb5.
- **48 bar 5 ("Herz")**: Bb5 read by script at +0.11 of a step and by
  eye with its accent; the alto staff below was not cross-read.
- Everything outside 48 bars 1-8 and 51-end is not in the tune and was
  not verified to this standard (pp. 196-203 were read for the sung
  lines during the lane, pp. 203 bar 1 to 208 not at all).
