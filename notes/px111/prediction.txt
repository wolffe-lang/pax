px111 — ptys on PAX: the prediction, written 2026-11-09 at base 4d899ea4 (pax trunk at launch),
before the first kernel change. The kernel is unchanged from 4d899ea4 on this branch; what is
committed before this file is the probe, its build tool, its suite, the checker, the recording rig
and Linux's recordings.

Sources, the only ones: pty(7), pts(4), ioctl_tty(2) and the TIOC*(2const) pages, termios(3),
posix_openpt(3), grantpt(3), unlockpt(3), ptsname(3), ttyname(3), credentials(7),
proc_pid_stat(5), proc_sys_kernel(5) (man-pages 6.7); POSIX.1-2024 XBD chapter 11 and XSH
posix_openpt, grantpt, unlockpt, ptsname, tcsetpgrp, tcgetsid; black-box runs of Linux 7.2.4 (a
lane host's installed kernel image, booted under QEMU/KVM with notes/px111/vminit.c as its first
program: devtmpfs at /dev, devpts at /dev/pts with no options, one CPU, no network, no disk); and
strace of the probe in a rootless Ubuntu 24.04 container (which calls glibc's pty functions make).
No kernel's source of any OS, no C library's, no shell's. pelt's own tree (the maintainer's) was
read for what it does with COLUMNS and SIGWINCH (src/sh/edit.lu, src/sh/signal.lu).

The probe: user/elf/ptyprobe.c (tools/mkpty), static glibc, sections O T C W H P M, and `drive`
(S). The recordings: tests/mpx5-pty.d/linux/{probe,session}-{root,user}.txt, by
notes/px111/rig/record.sh from the suite's own archives (the session's pelt, stty, cut and sleep
are the bytes PAX runs). Two recordings of the same binary (68913eea, 184e9d31) differ only in the
rows ruled R-px111-6 below.

A. What Linux answers (measured), by section

O  /dev/ptmx is chr 5:2 0666 root's; /dev/pts a file system of its own, dir 0755, inode 1;
   /dev/pts/ptmx chr 5:2 mode 0000 (devpts' default), inode 2; /dev/pts lists `. .. ptmx`;
   /proc/sys/kernel/pty/nr counts the ptys open (0, 1, 4, back to 0). A new master: TIOCGPTN 0,
   ptsname /dev/pts/0, TIOCGPTLCK 1 (locked); its slave chr 136:0, 0600, the opener's
   file-system uid and gid (0:0 as root, 1000:1000 as a user), inode 3 (N + 3); opening it while
   locked -EIO; grantpt 0 and changes nothing (no chown on devpts); unlockpt 0, TIOCGPTLCK 0; the
   slave opens; isatty 1 both; ttyname(slave) /dev/pts/0, ttyname(master) /dev/ptmx (its link);
   TIOCGPTN and TIOCSPTLCK on a slave -ENOTTY; TIOCGPTPEER opens the slave (136:0), on a slave
   -EIO; TIOCPKT 0; relocking keeps the open slave reading; numbers are the lowest free (1 2 3,
   close 2, the next is 2); a master closed under an open slave takes the slave's name away at once
   (ENOENT) and pty/nr drops when both ends are closed; a user opening root's slave -EACCES;
   posix_openpt opens /dev/ptmx; /dev/ptmx opened read-only refuses a write -EBADF. Past
   /proc/sys/kernel/pty/max: -ENOSPC (`ptyprobe max`, Linux only: with max 4, three opened).
T  A new pty's termios (slave and master read the same): iflag 0x500 (ICRNL|IXON), oflag 0x5,
   cflag 0xbf, lflag 0x8a3b, c_cc 3 28 127 21 4 0 1 0 17 19 26 0 18 15 23 22 0 0 0, B38400. The
   console's line discipline's answers throughout (canonical editing, VERASE/VKILL/VWERASE/VEOF,
   ECHOCTL's ^X, ECHONL, ICRNL/IGNCR/INLCR, OPOST/ONLCR on the slave's output, cfmakeraw, VMIN and
   VTIME), and: a part of a line is not readable (poll, FIONREAD 0); FIONREAD on the master counts
   the echo; settings set through the master are the slave's; with VMIN 3 two bytes are not
   readable under poll with VTIME 0 (they are with VTIME 5), but a non-blocking read takes them; tcflush on the slave of its
   output does nothing to what the master has not read, on the master of its input discards the
   slave's unread output, and TCOFLUSH on the master leaves the slave's input; TIOCOUTQ 0; IXON:
   after ^S a non-blocking write of the slave is -EAGAIN and the master reads nothing, after ^Q
   writes go through; ^S and ^Q in a line are not part of it.
C  A session leader with no terminal opening a slave without O_NOCTTY takes it: tcgetsid and
   tcgetpgrp are its own; tcgetpgrp(master) is the slave's foreground group (0 before any session);
   TIOCGSID on the master the slave's session (-ENOTTY while no session holds it); /dev/tty opens it (its own node, chr 5:0 0666, link
   /dev/tty, writes reach the master); /proc stat tty_nr 136:N and tpgid. TIOCSCTTY: its own 0,
   a second pty by a leader that has one -EPERM, a member that leads nothing -EPERM, another
   session's terminal -EPERM, root's arg 1 steals it with no signal to anyone (the thief's later
   TIOCNOTTY hangs up the thief's own group); tcsetpgrp: a child's group 0, a missing group
   -ESRCH, another session's -EPERM, through the master 0; ^C ^\ ^Z written to the master signal
   the foreground group (INT, QUIT, TSTP), echo `^C` and flush the line unless NOFLSH; ISIG clear
   makes them bytes; a background read is SIGTTIN (ignored: -EIO), a background write under TOSTOP
   and a background tcsetattr are SIGTTOU; TIOCNOTTY by a member loses it alone, by the leader
   hangs up the foreground group (HUP, CONT) and leaves the tty with no session; TIOCNOTTY on a
   master -ENOTTY.
W  A new pty is 0x0; TIOCSWINSZ on the master or the slave sets both sides' size and sends SIGWINCH
   to the foreground group, nothing when the size is unchanged, a change of the pixel fields alone
   counts; a bad pointer -EFAULT both ways; a pty no session holds takes a size and signals nobody.
H  Closing the master hangs up the slave: the controlling process (the session leader) hears HUP
   and CONT, the foreground group nothing; the slave then reads 0 (EOF, again and again), writes
   -EIO, polls IN OUT ERR HUP, tcgetpgrp/FIONREAD/tcgetattr -EIO, /dev/tty -ENXIO. Every slave
   descriptor closed: the master reads what is left, then -EIO, polls OUT HUP, and still takes
   writes (the slave opened again reads them, the echo comes back); a master whose slave was never
   opened polls OUT, reads -EAGAIN, takes writes. The session leader's end sends its foreground
   group SIGHUP (no CONT) and leaves the tty with no session (tcgetpgrp of the master 0, TIOCGSID
   -ENOTTY).
P  POLLOUT on both new ends; the echo makes the master readable; a line makes the slave readable;
   O_NONBLOCK reads -EAGAIN; full buffers: byte at a time, 20480 each way (P10a, P10b), larger
   writes 13824-22096 (varies, R-px111-6); blocking writers wait and every byte arrives intact
   (100000 each way); waiting readers and polls are woken by the other side's write; a canonical
   line holds 4095 bytes and its newline (5000 written, 4096 read).
M  64 pairs at once, numbered 0-63, each slave reads its own master's bytes, all closed: /dev/pts
   and pty/nr back to none; 1000 rounds of open/use/close always number 0, nothing left; a pair
   shared with a child that ended, then closed: nothing left.
S  pelt (/bin/sh -i) as the session leader of a pty the driver holds the master of: its prompt;
   `stty size` 0 0, after TIOCSWINSZ 30x100 `30 100`; a WINCH trap that sets COLUMNS from `stty
   size`, then TIOCSWINSZ 40x120: `echo cols=$COLUMNS` says 120; `sleep 100` becomes the
   foreground job (tcgetpgrp of the master moves), the byte 0x03 written to the master ends it,
   pelt has the terminal back and `$?` is 130; the master closed: pelt dies of SIGHUP. As root and
   as 1000:1000 alike.

D. The kernel, as it will be built (each a row above):
 1. One line discipline, several terminals (kernel/console). The console's state and ring are
    addressed through a terminal id: 0 the console (boot/console.S's storage, as today), 1 + n
    pty n's slave (its state and 4096-byte ring in frames the pty holds). Every routine of the
    discipline (input, echo, erase, the canonical and raw reads, VMIN/VTIME, termios in and out,
    the background rules, the signal keys) takes the id; echo and output processing go to COM1 for
    the console and to the pty's output queue for a slave. No second discipline.
 2. kernel/pty: 256 ptys (R-px111-4), a table of records in .bss; at the open of /dev/ptmx the
    lowest free number, 11 frames (state, the discipline's ring, a 16384-byte queue from the
    master to the discipline, a 20480-byte queue from the slave to the master), locked; the
    master's and the slave's open counts; given back when both are closed. The master's write
    queues bytes for the discipline and feeds it while it takes them (raw: room in the ring;
    canonical: a line still being edited takes bytes, dropped past 4095 as on the console; a full
    ring of whole lines stops the feed); the slave's read feeds it again. The slave's write goes
    through OPOST/ONLCR into the master's queue, waits (or -EAGAIN) when it is full or IXON has
    stopped it (R-px111-2). Signal keys written to the master are raised for that tty's foreground
    group at once (a system call, not an interrupt).
 3. /dev/ptmx (devtmpfs's 5:2 node, 0666) and /dev/pts (its own device number; `.`/`..`/`ptmx`
    and the open slaves' numbers, inode N + 3, mode 0600, the opener's fsuid/fsgid, rdev 136 +
    N/256 : N%256), with walk, stat, getdents, readlink of /proc/self/fd/N, access checks by the
    class rule, /dev/pts/ptmx opening a master too.
 4. Sessions: a process's controlling terminal is a terminal id (0 none, the console, a pty), and
    each terminal's session and foreground group are kernel/ttys' (one small table both
    kernel/sched and kernel/console use); setsid, TIOCSCTTY (no signal at a steal, R-px111-9),
    TIOCNOTTY, open without O_NOCTTY, /dev/tty, TIOCGPGRP/TIOCSPGRP/TIOCGSID (on a master: the
    slave's), the background rules, a leader's exit, /proc/<pid>/stat's tty_nr and tpgid, all per
    terminal.
 5. The ioctls: TCGETS/TCSETS*/TCFLSH/TCSBRK/TIOCSTI as the console's, TIOCGWINSZ/TIOCSWINSZ
    (SIGWINCH to the foreground group on a change), FIONREAD, TIOCOUTQ (0), TIOCGPTN, TIOCSPTLCK,
    TIOCGPTLCK, TIOCGPTPEER, TIOCSCTTY, TIOCNOTTY; everything else refused by name (TIOCPKT and the
    rest: R-px111-3).
 6. Hangup: the master's last close hangs the slave up (SIGHUP and SIGCONT to the controlling
    process, reads 0, writes and ioctls -EIO, poll IN OUT ERR HUP), the session loses the
    terminal; the slave's last close makes the master read what is left, then -EIO, poll HUP.
 7. poll: the masks above for both ends (VMIN under poll with VTIME 0), every wait woken by
    kernel/sched's pipe wake (`wake_pipes`) after any change to a pty.
 8. /proc/sys/kernel/pty/nr and max (R-px111-5).

E. Rulings asked (each implemented as recommended unless ruled otherwise)
 R-px111-1 devpts' options. A (recommended): Linux's defaults with no options: a slave 0600 owned
   by the opener's file-system ids, /dev/pts/ptmx 0000 (PAX has no tty group; nothing in its
   image writes to another's terminal). B: a distribution's gid=5,mode=620,ptmxmode=666, with a
   `tty` group added to every image.
 R-px111-2 IXON on a pty. A (recommended): Linux's default iflag 0x500 and IXON honoured on ptys
   (^S holds the slave's output and its writers, ^Q or IXON cleared releases it, both are taken
   out of the input); the console keeps px13's IXON clear. B: a pty starts with the console's
   0x100 and ^S/^Q are bytes (T1, T2, T36, T67-T71 ruled).
 R-px111-3 packet mode (TIOCPKT). A (recommended): refused by name, -ENOTTY and the kernel's
   line (no program PAX ships asks it; ssh servers that do are not ported). B: implement it.
 R-px111-4 how many ptys. A (recommended): 256 at once (11 frames each while open, 11 MiB at
   most), -ENOSPC past them as Linux past pty/max; /proc/sys/kernel/pty/max reads 256 and is not
   writable. B: 64. C: 4096 as Linux (44 MiB of frames at most).
 R-px111-5 /proc/sys/kernel/pty/nr and max served (the leak rows read nr). A (recommended): yes.
   B: no (M and O's nr rows ruled to ENOENT).
 R-px111-6 the full buffers' sizes. A (recommended): 20480 bytes each way, Linux's count for
   byte-at-a-time writes, held to Linux in P10a/P10b; the rows of larger writes, where Linux's
   count moves with the write size and the run, ruled to PAX's 20480 (and P10c's 20479, where
   Linux kept 12 bytes fewer than it took). B: any fixed size, every count ruled.
 R-px111-7 a failed open of a locked slave. A (recommended): it changes nothing for descriptors
   already open (O40, O43 ruled). B: Linux's: the open descriptors' next read -EIO.
 R-px111-8 what a pty refuses. A (recommended): by name, every request but section D.5's
   (TIOCPKT, TIOCSIG, TIOCEXCL/NXCL, TIOCCONS, TIOCSETD/GETD, TIOCVHANGUP, TCXONC, the KD*
   requests), as the console's REFUSED path does. B: implement TIOCSIG and TCXONC too.
 R-px111-9 TIOCSCTTY's steal. A (recommended): no signal to anyone at a steal (C58-C63, measured
   here), for the console too: px48's `take_tty` sent the old foreground group SIGHUP and SIGCONT,
   from a measurement whose `R:CONT,R:HUP` was the thief's own TIOCNOTTY under a tag it inherited
   (jobs.c's thief keeps R's tag and handlers), so mpx5-jobs' transcript is unchanged by the fix.
   B: keep px48's signals (C60 ruled).
 R-px111-10 the session row's $COLUMNS. pelt sets no COLUMNS of its own (XBD 8.3: it reads COLUMNS
   as the user's width), so A (recommended): the resize reaches $COLUMNS through a WINCH trap typed
   in the session (SIGWINCH to the foreground group, the trap, stty's TIOCGWINSZ), as recorded.
   B: a pelt lane to set COLUMNS on SIGWINCH as bash's checkwinsize does, and the row after it.
 R-px111-11 the console. A (recommended): the console stays one terminal (ttyS0, tty0, console:
   one line discipline instance, one session), its answers unchanged; only its state is addressed
   by id. B: also give the screen and COM1 terminals of their own.
