Title: paxfat cat of a 545 MB file runs for minutes at 100% CPU before writing a byte

What happened (2026-11-04, satoyama, px92's dev stick comparison): the release pair's own
`sout/disktools/paxfat --offset 2097152 cat <dev stick image> /boot/initramfs.cpio` (the dev archive,
545,376,324 bytes, on the stick's FAT32 ESP) ran 6 min 48 s at 99.7% of one CPU with an RSS of about 80 MB.
/proc/<pid>/io read twice 10 s apart: rchar 17,135,841 both times, syscr 24, wchar 0. It had read 17 MB in
24 reads and written nothing to its stdout pipe. Stopped by its pid. The same file's sha256 came from
tools/check-usb's U2 in seconds (the expected digest matched).

Why it matters: not on the installer's path (pax-install copies the running root; Limine loads the archive),
but `paxfat cat` is how the lanes and the metal legs read files off a stick (the install log, the kernel for the
screen's symbols, the allow-list), and a large file looks hung.

Expected: a 545 MB file out in about the time its clusters take to read (seconds), or output streamed as it is
read.

Not measured: the time it would have taken to finish, or where the CPU goes (cluster-chain walk per cluster,
an output buffer built by appending, or something else). A reproducer: tools/mkdisk --usb with any release
archive over ~400 MB, then `paxfat --offset <ESP byte> cat IMAGE /boot/initramfs.cpio | sha256sum`, timed
against the archive's size.
