1. The red row and which clock it reads

CI's red at 584c5313 (run 42459307387, job 128665265213):
  FAIL D1 native bios expiry (virtio): pcap-dhcp: FAIL frame 30 (+122.511):
  a DISCOVER while a lease is held (it runs out at +123.549)
tools/pcap-dhcp fails a DISCOVER more than 1.0 s before the lease's end.

kernel/dhcp counts its lease, T1 and T2 in timer.ticks(): the local APIC
timer's interrupts, 100 a second. The tick's length is set once, at boot:
timer.start counts the LAPIC timer down across clock.wait_us(20000), and
before this change it read the clock, then the count; the count, then
the clock. So the clock's interval held the LAPIC's, and the time the
reads themselves took was in the clock's interval alone. The rate came
out low, the tick short, and every timeout counted in ticks early by
the same fraction.

The guest's own interval from lease to `expired` (its ticks: 120.000 s
every time) against the capture's from ACK to the next DISCOVER, in
every expiry boot of two CI artifacts (bin/dhcp-clock.py):

  boot                         calibrated (Hz)  capture/guest  implied rate (Hz)
  584c5313 native BIOS            61,940,478      0.99135       62,480,650
  ab3a1827 native BIOS            61,188,519      0.97915       62,491,275
  ab3a1827 native UEFI            62,116,863      0.99402       62,490,527
  ab3a1827 release BIOS           62,235,490      0.99590       62,491,498
  ab3a1827 release UEFI           62,229,499      0.99581       62,491,090

Each boot implies the same LAPIC rate to 0.02% whatever its calibration
read. The tick ran ahead of real time by exactly the calibration's
shortfall, and the clock (the HPET) read true. Under KVM the paired
calibration below reads 62,499,225-62,500,183 Hz (m5a).

4. The fix's first half: paired, warmed reads (dh4)

Each end of the window reads the count and then the clock back to back
through one function, after one read to warm the path, so the reads'
own time is in both intervals and cancels. The same take, the row's
commit (the old calibration) against the paired one:

  D1 expiry red                4 of 9                0 of 9

                                                     (62,498,469-62,506,753)

5. A boot that calibrated 1.433 times high

The install image's kernel (this pull request's clock) under KVM: 1 boot
in 19 printed `timer: LAPIC calibrated by HPET divide16 89527688 Hz`
(the others 62.45-62.49 MHz), and its clock, sleeps and timeouts ran at
0.70 of real time: `sleep 5` took 7.168 s by the host against 5.03 s by
the guest's `date` (its serial log's sha256 b3f00c0d...).

That boot is unexplained. A stall between two reads does not give it:
in the old order the clock's interval holds the LAPIC's, so a stall
anywhere can only make the rate low. Tested: 20 KVM boots of the session
kernel at 584c5313 with the vCPU's CPU shared with a busy loop (m5a) all
calibrated at 62.472-62.476 MHz. A rate 1.433 times high needs one
counter to have moved unevenly against the other inside the window, in
one of two shapes: the clock stood still (for about 8.6 ms of a 20 ms
window), or the LAPIC's count jumped. Which of the two that boot met is
not known, and nothing here reproduces it unprompted. The calibration
below catches either: a clock that stood still by the PM timer
(`timer: LAPIC calibration: window <k> set aside: …`), a count that
jumped by the next window (`timer: LAPIC calibration: windows <j> and <k>
disagree: …`).

timer-stall (tests/mpx6-metal) makes the clock stand still 8.6 ms inside
the calibration (one HPET reading 8.6 ms ahead, then the readings behind
it taken as no time passing) and times 50 ticks by the PM timer read
directly (lt1, BIOS):

                    old (row)           paired              windows
  KVM calibrated    89,281,777 Hz       89,287,452 Hz       62,505,550 Hz
  KVM 50 ticks      714,232 us          714,373 us          500,017 us
  TCG calibrated    88,057,666 Hz       88,037,568 Hz       62,501,880 Hz
  TCG 50 ticks      703,747 us          703,610 us          499,339 us
  the row           red                 red                 green

At the windows commit both lines are on the console:
  timer: LAPIC calibration: window 1 set aside: 89274972 Hz, the clock 20008 us against the PM timer's 28581 us
  timer: LAPIC calibration: windows 2 and 3 of 6 agree

The fix's second half: 20 ms windows until two in a row agree within
0.1% (their mean); a window whose clock interval is not the PM timer's
within 0.5% is set aside (when a PM timer stands beside the HPET); each
set-aside window and each disagreement is a boot line; after six, the
median of the last three kept. A clock that stands still is caught by
the PM timer; a LAPIC count that jumps, by the next window's
disagreement.

6. On real hardware

Under KVM the old bracket read 62.472-62.476 MHz against 62.500 (m5a):
0.04% short, where every HPET read is a trip to the emulator. A hardware
read costs no more than that, so before this change the shortfall on
hardware is under 0.04% from the reads, if no stall falls inside the
window. After it the reads' time cancels on any machine, and what
remains is the window's resolution (20 ms of a 14.318 MHz HPET is 70 ns,
3.5 ppm; the LAPIC's count, one in 20 ms of its rate) and the two
counters' crystals drifting apart after the calibration, which these
takes cannot measure without the hardware. A stall on hardware (an
SMI inside a window) is set aside like any other.

7. With no PM timer beside the clock

Then the agreement rule works alone, and the boot says so first:
  timer: LAPIC calibration: no second counter beside the HPET to hold each window to; two windows off alike in a row would pass
timer-stall-nopm (the same stall, the PM timer hidden from the
calibration by a test word) at the head, lt2:
  KVM: windows 1 and 2 disagree: 87048098 and 62499001 Hz; windows 2 and
       3 of 6 agree; calibrated 62,499,825 Hz; 50 ticks in 499,914 us
  TCG: windows 1 and 2 disagree: 84219705 and 62500881 Hz; windows 2 and
       3 of 6 agree; calibrated 62,501,552 Hz; 50 ticks in 499,374 us
So a counter that stands still or jumps in one window is caught without
a PM timer too. What it cannot catch: the same disturbance, alike within
0.1%, in two windows in a row, which the boot line names. The line prints
only when the clock is the HPET: with the PM timer or the PIT as the clock
the agreement rule works alone as well, and the clock's own `clock:` line
names the one counter there is. (At first it printed for those too, and CI
found it: mpx2-interrupts' I5 holds the timer kernel's last four lines on a
machine without an HPET, and the new line sat among them.)

None high: under these loads the stick's code calibrated normally in 26
boots (12, 2 and 12), the head within 0.05% of 62.5 MHz in 25, and the
trigger is not reproduced. The load did reach the emulated
counters: a KVM boot run beside m6b had its HPET refused by the clock's
own check against the PM timer (`no window measured cleanly against the
PM timer in 3; refused`, 192 brackets taken again), the shape of pax#314.

A stall of the emulator could hold the HPET's read and the PM timer's
alike, so the PM timer would not see it. timer-stall-shared has the PM
timer answer from the clock (it stands still with it); the agreement rule
must catch the stalled window (lt5, at the head):

  TCG  windows 1 and 2 disagree: 87394974 and 62500518 Hz; 2 and 3 agree;
       calibrated 62,501,146 Hz; 50 ticks in 499,443 us
and timer-stall and timer-stall-nopm green beside it in the same take.

The KVM expiry boot's D1 red is the capture's reading, not the client's.
That boot ran beside m6c's write load: dnsmasq answered the first REQUEST
late, the client asked again 4 s later, and the server ACKed both (frames
15 and 20, +9.048 and +10.997). The client bound on the first, and its
lease, T1, T2 and expiry count from it: the DISCOVER at +129.403 is 0.36 s
after that lease's end (+129.048), late by the ticks lost under the load.
tools/pcap-dhcp took the lease from the last ACK (+10.997, so +130.997)
and called the DISCOVER 1.6 s early. RFC 2131 has the client bind on the
first ACK and drop the rest; the capture's rule should take the lease from
that one.
