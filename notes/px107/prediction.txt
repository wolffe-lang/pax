px107 — the clock reads forward: the prediction, written before any measurement
(2026-11-07 23:15Z, at trunk db516c69, branch px107).

Item 1 (pax#293). kernel/clock's now_ns panics `overflow ./clock/clock.lu:313`
when a reading of a 64-bit counter is below the one before: the wrap branch
turns the step back into a delta near 2^64, and (delta / 10^6) * period
overflows. Three witnesses, all TCG soaks on CI (41805401996 at efe12e51,
42055543811 at 411fc9f5, the chk-soak-user boot; 42403298471 at db516c69,
the rights-soak boot, `clock: HPET 10000000 fs/tick, checked against the PM
timer`).

What was read before writing this (no measurement):
  - PAX runs one CPU. now_ns reads raw() and `previous` with IF clear
    (sync.irq_save), and nothing else writes `previous` after start. The NMI
    handler (kernel/interrupts) reads no clock. Nothing writes the HPET after
    try_hpet's one write of ENABLE_CNF.
  - raw() on the native tier is one 8-byte load: `mov (%rax),%rax` at
    HPET_VA + 0xf0 (objdump of a native kmain_console built from a tree whose
    kernel/clock is trunk's).
  - IA-PC HPET 1.0a §2.4.7, the note: "On a 64-bit platform, if software
    attempts a 64 bit read of the 64-bit counter, software must be aware that
    some platforms may break the 64 bit read into two 32 bit reads, and
    therefore the read may be inaccurate if the low 32 bits roll over between
    the high and low part reads." The section gives the safe read: high,
    low, high again, repeated until the two highs agree.

R-px107-1, the cause, predicted: (A) the read is torn. QEMU's HPET (a black
box) answers the 8-byte load as two 4-byte reads taken at different
instants, and when the low word rolls over between them the value is one
high word (2^32 ticks, 42.9 s at 10 ns) off.
  - Every backward reading on the HPET (source 1, mask 0xffffffffffffffff)
    has prev - current within 2^24 ticks (168 ms) of 2^32: the high words one
    apart, the low words on the two sides of a rollover. None is a reset
    (current below the uptime's ticks with no 2^32 shape) and none is of
    another size.
  - Each comes with a forward step of about 2^32 ticks next to it: before it
    if the device reads the low half first (the torn reading is ahead), after
    it if it reads the high half first (the torn reading is behind).
  - Not (B): nothing restarts the counter; not (C): no second CPU, no
    interrupt between the two reads of now_ns, no store of `previous`
    elsewhere.

The rate, predicted:
  - The rights soak (`/bin/rights soak 100000`, TCG, the image mpx5-users
    builds) reads the clock about 100 times a second (the tick), and a torn
    read needs the two halves to straddle a rollover, one every 42.9 s. In
    the soaks this lane can run (20 or more, about 10 minutes each under
    TCG): 0 or 1 backward readings. The rate is below 1 in 10 soaks; if none
    shows, the note says how many soaks saw none, and the fix stands on the
    arithmetic and on the spec.
  - A hammer boot (now_ns in a tight loop for 600 s of the clock's own time,
    13 rollovers or more) under TCG: at least one backward reading of that
    shape. Under KVM: at least one too, since the split is the device's
    answer, not the translator's.

The fix, predicted:
  - raw() reads a 64-bit HPET as §2.4.7 says: high, low, high, again until
    the highs agree (bounded); a 32-bit HPET's one 4-byte read of 0xf0.
  - now_ns: on a 64-bit mask a reading below `previous` is no time passing:
    elapsed unchanged, `previous` unchanged (the counter's high-water mark),
    the reading counted and its operands kept (current, previous, mask,
    source). On a narrower mask (the PIT, the PM timer, a 32-bit HPET) it is
    still a wrap and counts forward.
  - The arithmetic bounded per source so no implicit overflow check can
    fire; any check left names current, previous and the mask.
  - Each kernel grows by under 2 KiB of ELF a tier.

The rows, predicted:
  - The backward-read row (a test word: the next reading of a 64-bit HPET
    taken 2^32 - 7 ticks behind the one before), committed before the fix:
    at the rows' commit it ends `PANIC overflow ./clock/clock.lu:<the line
    of elapsed's sum>`; after the fix the two answers around it are
    monotonic, and the count of backward readings is 1.
  - A true rollover of a 32-bit HPET and of the PM timer (test words that
    put the counter just before its rollover) count forward correctly at
    the rows' commit and after the fix: green at both.
  - The bound row (the largest step each source can produce, through the
    arithmetic): green after the fix.
  - No existing row moves: tests/mpx6-firmware's F1 (mpx6-metal), the
    interrupt and scheduler suites, mpx5-users.
