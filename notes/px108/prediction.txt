px108 prediction, written 2026-11-08T05:20Z before any change and before the
first measurement (base pax trunk db516c69). Revised once before commit for
the second set of metal timings (https 85 s, the same as ssh's 86 s; a local
file:// clone failing after 48 s with `den: wire: limit` then
`den: wire: invalid`).

What the metal saw (a stick booting kmain_console; root the in-memory tree;
den at user/den.pin, ac2332b; burrow at user/burrow.pin, 0d98ce9):
pelt (12,670 objects, a 4.4 MB pack, 83 refs) cloned over ssh in 86 s and
over https in 85 s; git on another machine in 2.4 s.

What the pinned den does for a clone (read from den's tree at ac2332b, the
org's own code): wiregit/fetch.lu's fetch_view reads the whole response,
decodes the whole pack in memory (wiregit/objects.lu import_pack), then
installs every object LOOSE through object/store.lu's write_object:
repository.open (realpath, four probes, the config read and parsed), the
object's SHA-1, a probe for an existing copy (an open that misses, then a
look in objects/pack), mkdir -p, deflate, an O_EXCL temporary, write,
fsync, close, link, unlink, and an fsync of two directories: about 25
system calls an object. Then closure() reads every reachable object back
with a bound of 10,000 objects. den trunk since vc23 (474a926) streams the
pack to disk and indexes it instead (den#61); the 1.32x-git figure on Linux
is that den, not the stick's.

P1. The dominant cost is den's per-object work at the pin, through PAX's
    system calls and memory on one CPU. It is transport-independent, which
    is why https and ssh agree to a second and file:// spends 48 s with no
    network at all. Not the pipe hand-off (suspect 1), not TCP (2), not
    crypto (3), not fork (4), not the disk (5: the stick's root is the
    tree, and fsync of a tree inode answers 0 at once).

P2. The pinned den cannot clone pelt on any kernel: closure()'s
    `out.len >= 10000` bound fires at the 10,000th of pelt's 12,670
    reachable objects. Over file:// the serving side (upload-pack's
    closure) fires it, `den: wire: limit`, and the client reports the cut
    stream, `den: wire: invalid`: the 860's two lines. Over https and ssh
    the client's closure after the import fires it, and the failed clone
    removes its directory. Predicted the same on Linux at this pin; den
    trunk passes. (If the 860's https clone printed no error, P2 is wrong
    for https and the 85 s is a whole clone.)

P3. The split for pelt under KVM, PAX on one CPU, the pinned den, https from
    a loopback fixture (seconds; the Linux 1-vCPU guest in brackets):
      connect, TLS, ls-refs                      < 0.5      [< 0.2]
      the pack over TCP, TLS decrypted          0.5 - 3     [< 0.5]
      the pack decoded in memory (user time)      2 - 6     [1.5 - 5]
      12,670 loose installs (~320,000 calls)     30 - 50    [2 - 5]
      closure to the bound (~50,000 calls)       10 - 20    [0.5 - 2]
      the failed clone's removal                  2 - 5     [< 1]
    Total 50 - 80 s on PAX, 10 - 20x the Linux guest for the same den.
    den's own CPU (user + system) at least 85 % of the wall on PAX: it is
    working, not waiting. System time at least half of den's CPU on PAX,
    under a fifth on Linux.

P4. Per system call PAX costs 100 - 200 us on that mix against Linux's
    2 - 10 us. The suspects inside, ranked:
      (a) memory: anonymous memory zero-filled and mapped at the call
          (mmap, brk up), where Linux maps lazily, and a walk of the whole
          user page table before every munmap and brk down (px88's
          note_peak): paid on each of glibc malloc's map/unmap or
          grow/trim cycles, which a per-object allocation of 64 KiB and up
          makes; and the frames given back one by one;
      (b) path walks and directory records in kernel/tree (realpath's
          lstat per component, objects/xx lookups), and the open-file
          table;
      (c) the system-call path itself (the bracket, the drain after every
          call, wake_pipes' scan of 64 slots).
    (a) first.

P5. The microbenchmarks, PAX against the Linux 1-vCPU guest under the
    same QEMU and KVM:
      pipe ping-pong, 1 B: PAX within 3x the guest's round trips a second
        (a blocked reader runs as soon as the writer blocks; no quantum is
        waited out); 64 KiB: within 3x;
      TCP from the host into the guest: PAX 10 - 20 MB/s (px89: 15.1 MB/s
        in over igb) against the guest's 100+; with `netpoll` (the metal
        I225's polled path) at most 64 KiB a tick, about 6 MB/s;
      chacha20-poly1305 (burrow) and wolf-std's TLS records in-process:
        the same on both kernels (user code), 20 - 100 MB/s;
      small-file create+write+fsync+close: PAX 3 - 10x slower;
      a malloc/free cycle of 256 KiB (map and unmap): PAX 20x+ slower.

P6. The fix in PAX, if P4 (a) holds: keep the peak without the whole walk
    (count the pages as they are mapped and unmapped: R-px88-3's B) and
    give frames back without per-page cost; possibly anonymous memory on
    first touch. Predicted: the pinned den's pelt work 2 - 4x faster on
    PAX, still several times the guest, the rest den's own (the loose
    path), which is den's to change (move the pin to den trunk: a den or
    pax-pin lane, not this one).

P7. With den trunk (streamed pack, no loose installs) the same clone is
    5 - 10x faster on PAX than with the pin, and TCP's 64 KiB window (no
    window scaling) and the polled I225 become the largest PAX share for
    den's own 311 MB pack ("a few minutes": 311 MB at 2 - 6 MB/s is
    50 - 150 s).

P8. SMP is not the next lever for pelt: one CPU costs at most 2x here
    (git's delta resolution is the only parallel stage).
