px109 — the mouse: the prediction, written before the first change (2026-11-09)

Base: pax trunk db516c699e11. Written from USB HID 1.11 (§6.2.1, §6.2.2,
§7.2.4, §7.2.6, appendix B.2), HID Usage Tables 1.7 (§4 Generic Desktop,
§12 Button), xHCI 1.2 (§6.2.3.6, interrupt endpoints) and QEMU's
documentation (system/devices/usb, the QMP reference's input-send-event,
InputButton, InputMoveEvent, the monitor's mouse_set and info mice). No
device model's, driver's or kernel's source. Every P-line below is scored
in the note against what the measurement and the rows show.

A. What QEMU's devices present (measured over PAX's own xHCI stack, as a
   black box: the descriptors read by the driver and logged in hex, and,
   with `usbraw` on the command line, every interrupt report's bytes).

P1  usb-mouse enumerates at full speed (xHCI speed 1) on qemu-xhci, with
    bMaxPacketSize0 8; its vendor is 0627, its product string names a
    mouse.
P2  usb-mouse's configuration has one interface: class 3, subclass 1
    (boot), protocol 2 (mouse); a HID descriptor (type 21h, bcdHID 0101 or
    0111) naming one report descriptor (type 22h) of 40 to 80 bytes; one
    endpoint 81h, interrupt, wMaxPacketSize 4 to 8, bInterval 10 (or a
    smaller power-of-two-friendly value).
P3  usb-mouse's report descriptor is the boot mouse of appendix B.2 with
    a wheel: Generic Desktop Mouse application, Pointer physical, Button
    usages 1 to 3 (1 bit each, then 5 bits of padding), X, Y and Wheel as
    8-bit relative fields with logical extents -127..127; no Report ID.
    Its report is 4 bytes: buttons, dx, dy, wheel.
P4  usb-tablet enumerates at full speed too, interface class 3, subclass
    0, protocol 0 (no boot protocol: an absolute device is not a boot
    mouse).
P5  usb-tablet's report descriptor: buttons 1 to 3 (and 5 bits of
    padding), X and Y as 16-bit ABSOLUTE fields with logical extents
    0..32767, the wheel an 8-bit relative field; no Report ID; a 6-byte
    report: buttons, x lo, x hi, y lo, y hi, wheel.
P6  Reports for injected events (QMP input-send-event):
      rel x +10            usb-mouse 00 0a 00 00
      rel y +5             usb-mouse 00 00 05 00
      btn left down/up     01 00 00 00, then 00 00 00 00 (right 02, middle 04)
      btn wheel-up         wheel +1 (byte 3 = 01); wheel-down -1 (ff)
      abs x 20000 y 400    usb-tablet 00 20 4e 90 01 00
    side and extra (buttons 4 and 5) reach neither device: both describe
    three buttons.
P7  A relative move larger than 127 in one event is split by the device
    over several reports (clamped to the field, the rest carried), so the
    sum of the reports is the sum injected.
P8  Under the boot protocol (SET_PROTOCOL 0) usb-mouse keeps sending the
    same 4-byte report (the appendix allows device-specific bytes after
    the third).
P9  Routing: rel events reach the usb-mouse, abs events the usb-tablet;
    btn events reach the mouse `info mice` marks current, and `mouse_set`
    moves that mark between the two. With two usb-mouse devices, rel
    events reach only the current one.
P10 The devices report only on change: no report while nothing moves
    (no idle repeat after SET_IDLE 0).
P11 On trunk both devices are addressed and configured far enough to be
    called "not driven" (no line on the log), and /dev/mouse0 does not
    exist (open answers -ENOENT).

B. What each change will show.

P12 kernel/usb_mouse (new, pure): the boot report decoded per B.2; a
    report descriptor parsed only as far as X, Y, Wheel and Buttons 1 to
    5 need (Usage Page, Usage, Usage Minimum/Maximum, Logical
    Minimum/Maximum, Report Size/Count/ID, Input, Collection, End
    Collection; Push, Pop, long items and Delimiter refused by name, as is
    a descriptor with no X or Y, a field wider than 32 bits, or a report
    longer than 64 bytes). QEMU's two descriptors parse; so do five
    synthetic real-mouse shapes (16-bit X/Y with a Report ID, 12-bit
    packed X/Y, five buttons, a wheel and AC Pan, a keyboard-and-mouse
    descriptor with two Report IDs).
P13 kernel/mouse (new): one bounded queue per reader (126 records of 32
    bytes in one page; at most 8 readers): appended while under half full,
    motion merged into the tail above half, adjacent motion compacted when
    full, a button edge never merged; an edge is lost only when the queue
    holds no two adjacent motion records (a named counter, and a LOST flag
    on the next record).
P14 kernel/xhci drives a boot mouse (SET_PROTOCOL 0, SET_IDLE 0) and a
    report-descriptor pointer (the tablet), a pointer interface beside a
    keyboard interface on one device, hot-plug and unplug as the keyboard
    does (an unplug with a button held delivers its release), two or more
    at once; nothing in the keyboard path changes.
P15 /dev/mouse0 (kernel/files): present when an xHCI controller is;
    read takes whole 32-byte records (a shorter buffer -EINVAL), waits
    when empty (O_NONBLOCK -EAGAIN), poll and epoll POLLIN; root's, 0600.
P16 kmain_console grows by 12 to 24 KiB per tier (native ELF).
P17 mpx6-usbcheck's 17 invariant rows stay; about 30 mouse rows join
    them.
P18 The existing keyboard rows (mpx6-usb's 12 sessions, the typing
    replays) do not move.

C. The rows (tools/mouse-session over user/elf/mouseprobe.c; KVM and TCG,
   BIOS and UEFI, native and release).

P19 On trunk plus the rows, every mouse row is red by name (FAIL
    mouse-open first: /dev/mouse0 is absent), and the keyboard rows stay
    green.
P20 With the driver: moves, each button, a drag and the wheel on both
    devices green; the burst row (reader stopped for 3 s while about 400
    motions and 10 clicks arrive) keeps all 20 edges in order with the
    motion's sum exact and at least one MERGED record; unplug with left
    held gives a GONE record with buttons 0; a replug gets a new device
    number; keys typed during motion arrive byte-exact.
P21 Under TCG the rows take 2 to 4 times as long as under KVM; no row
    needs a longer deadline than the keyboard's (120 s a boot).
