C1  The rule for time today: CLOCK_MONOTONIC, CLOCK_REALTIME, every sleep and every timeout count delivered ticks
    (kernel/timer's count, one a tick interrupt). A tick due while interrupts are off waits, and every further one in
    the same stretch is lost, so each clock stands still for the length of a long call less 10 ms.
C2  Beside 64 KiB console writes (about 0.37 s each with interrupts off, COM1 present) CLOCK_MONOTONIC and
    CLOCK_REALTIME will keep about 8 % of the time-stamp counter's time over a 3 s window; beside 64 MiB file writes
    about a quarter. A bound of 2 % will be red by more than tenfold.
C3  The fix: the tick count follows kernel/clock. Each delivered tick sets the count to the larger of the count plus
    one and the ticks the clock says are due since the tick was armed; the interrupts themselves stay counted apart
    (the timer's drift report, the instrument's lost ticks). Every deadline in ticks (sleeps, poll, epoll, a receive's
    timeout, futexes, TCP, ARP, DHCP, a tone) is then a deadline in real time: a long call makes it late by at most
    the call, never by the call times the ticks lost. Both clocks then keep the counter's time within one tick
    (10 ms over 3 s: 0.3 %). A kernel that fakes its clock's readings for its own rows (kmain_metal) counts
    interrupts as before.
W1  The rule for wake-ups today: the device's interrupt queues netd at the tail; a switch happens only from idle or
    after the running thread's two-tick quantum; netd's deliveries queue their readers at the tail again. Beside a
    process that only computes, a host's ping waits about a quantum a hop: p50 about 27 ms, p99 about 36 ms, against
